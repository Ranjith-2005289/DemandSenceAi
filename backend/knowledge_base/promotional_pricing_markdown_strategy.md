# Promotional Pricing and Markdown/Clearance Strategy

**Promotions** temporarily boost demand but usually "borrow" some sales
from the weeks immediately before and after — this is called demand
cannibalization or pull-forward. A promotion that shows 40% higher units
during the event may only represent a 10-15% true incremental lift once
the pre/post dip is accounted for. When evaluating whether a past
promotion worked, always compare against a baseline that excludes the
promo window, not just the average of the surrounding weeks (which is
itself depressed by the promo).

**Markdown cadence** for slow-moving or end-of-season stock typically
follows a staged discount pattern (e.g. 20% → 40% → 60% off) rather than
one deep cut, because a single early deep discount can train customers to
wait for it next time, while too-slow markdowns leave capital tied up in
unsold inventory and increase the risk of needing an even deeper final
cut. A common heuristic: if sell-through rate (units sold ÷ units
received) is tracking below plan at the season's midpoint, escalate the
markdown a stage early rather than waiting for the scheduled date.

**Signs a product needs a markdown, not just patience:**
- Weeks of supply (current stock ÷ average weekly sales) is rising while
  competitors' equivalent items are turning over faster.
- Sell-through is meaningfully behind the same point in the product's
  life cycle last season (for recurring seasonal items) or behind
  category benchmark (for new items).
- The forecast's own trend component is flat or declining and the
  product is approaching a hard deadline (season end, shelf reset,
  contract expiration) where unsold stock becomes a total loss or
  requires liquidation at a much worse margin.

**Signs a declining trend is a pricing/assortment problem, not a
demand-planning problem:** if a forecast shows a genuine downward trend
(not just noise) but the trend started at a specific date that lines up
with a price increase, a competitor's entry, a stockout that broke
customer habit, or a promotional cadence change, the root cause is
usually the business action, not "worse demand" in the abstract — check
for anniversary-date correlation with pricing/assortment history before
assuming the market itself has shrunk.
